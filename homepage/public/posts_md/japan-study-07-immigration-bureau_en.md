# 07 | Studying in Japan #7: The Immigration Bureau — Applying for Permission to Work Part-Time

After finishing the various procedures at the ward office, one more important task remained: going to the **Immigration Services Bureau (入管局)** to apply for **permission to engage in activity other than that permitted under the status of residence** (資格外活動許可). For students who want to work part-time outside class, this is an essential step.

## Preparation: Booking Online

The immigration bureau is known for long waits; if you turn up without a booking, you can easily spend half a day or even a whole day waiting. So soon after arriving, I used the bureau's **online reservation system** to book a 9 a.m. slot.

Even so, I arrived at the bureau's door at 8 a.m. that morning, and plenty of people were already waiting outside. If you do not have a booking, you should arrive even earlier; I recommend at least one to two hours ahead.

## Application Process

### Step 1: Fill in the application form

At the bureau, pick up and fill in the "**Application for Permission to Engage in Activity Other Than That Permitted under the Status of Residence Previously Granted**" (資格外活動許可申請書, Form 28, relating to Article 19).

![Application for permission to engage in activity outside one's status of residence (Form 28)](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10442100.png)
*The application form: you fill in your nationality, name, residence card number, current activities (school name and weekly class hours), the work you plan to do and its weekly hours, and so on*

Pay special attention to item 11, "現在の在留活動の内容" (current activities in Japan), where students enter their school name and weekly class hours; item 12 is for the type of work you plan to do and your expected weekly hours (the cap for students is 28 hours).

### Step 2: Submit at the reservation counter on the second floor

Once the form is filled in, go to the **reservation-only counter on the second floor** to submit it. Documents needed:
- The completed application form
- Residence card
- Passport

After submission, the staff give you a **receipt**, along with a **postcard** you fill in on the spot and hand in with your application. When the bureau has finished its review, it posts this postcard to tell you to come and collect the result.

### Step 3: Wait for notification

About **two weeks** after applying, I received the notification postcard saying the result was ready to collect.

However, the postcard arrived just as Golden Week began and the bureau was closed, so I could only go after the holiday ended.

## Collection Day: A Long Wait

On collection day I arrived at the bureau at about **9 a.m.**, took a number ticket, and queued at **counter A** on the second floor.

The wait was surprisingly long: I waited **about six hours**, until about 3 p.m., for my turn. I recommend getting lunch after taking your number, keeping an eye on the number display, and **setting aside a whole day**.

## Collecting the Result

When your number is called, take the notification postcard, residence card and passport to counter A.

![The result notice issued by the immigration bureau](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10468341.png)
*The bureau's collection notice: bring your passport, residence card and so on to counter A1 on the second floor; queues may be long around Golden Week*

After checking everything, the staff returned my residence card, with the "permission to engage in activity outside status" field on the back stamped: **"許可　原則週28時間以内・風俗営業等の従事を除く"** (Permitted: in principle within 28 hours a week, excluding adult entertainment businesses). A permission sticker was also put in my passport.

![Back of the residence card, with the permission field stamped "許可　原則週28時間以内"](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10222262.png)
*The back of the residence card: the "許可" stamp formally confirms permission to work up to 28 hours a week, and the address field has been updated to a Tokyo address*

The moment this card was back in my hand, it felt like the foundation for my life in Japan was finally in place.

## Getting to the Immigration Bureau

The bureau is near Shinagawa. Take the **JR to Shinagawa Station**, then the **Shinagawa 99 bus** (品川99), which has stop announcements in English and Japanese.

## Summary

The whole application for permission to work part-time spans about a month, from submitting the application to waiting for notification to collecting it in person, and takes no small amount of time and energy. I recommend applying as soon as possible after arriving, using the online reservation system, and leaving plenty of waiting time on collection day. With permission in hand, another door of student life officially opens.

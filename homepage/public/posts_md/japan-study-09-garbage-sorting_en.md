# 09 | Studying in Japan #9: A Complete Guide to Sorting Rubbish in Japan — Using Shibuya as an Example

Living in Japan, there is one thing almost every newcomer needs time to get used to: **the rules for sorting and disposing of rubbish**. Compared with many countries, Japan's sorting system is strict and detailed. If you do not follow the rules, you may not only get looks from the neighbours; your rubbish may simply not be collected.

Below, using **Shibuya ward** where I currently live as an example, I have put together the full sorting and collection rules. Details vary by ward, so check against the guide for your own area.

---

## Basic Rule: When to Put Rubbish Out

Shibuya requires rubbish to be placed at the designated spot by **8:00 a.m.** on collection day in **regular areas**, and by **7:30 a.m.** in **busy commercial areas**. If you miss the time, you have to wait until the next collection.

---

## 1. Burnable Rubbish (可燃ごみ) — Twice a Week

This is the largest category in daily life, including:

- **Food waste (生ごみ)**: kitchen scraps and leftovers; drain off the water first
- **Paper scraps and clothing**: tissues, kitchen paper and other paper (except recyclable paper), worn-out clothes
- **Used cooking oil**: frying oil and other waste cooking oil, soaked into absorbent material such as paper or cloth
- **Plastics that cannot be cleaned**: plastic containers that cannot be washed (such as oily sachets)
- **Rubber and leather**: rubber and leather goods
- **Garden clippings**: branches from the garden, up to 50cm on each side, at most 4 bundles at a time

How to put it out: use a **lidded container** or a **see-through bag**.

## 2. Non-burnable Rubbish (不燃ごみ) — Once a Month

Only once a month, so do not miss it. It mainly includes:

- **Small metal items and metal hangers**: small metal objects and metal coat hangers (shorter than 30cm)
- **Batteries, incandescent bulbs, LED bulbs and fluorescent tubes**: ordinary batteries and all kinds of bulbs
- **Ceramics and glass**: ceramic and glass items (wrap broken ones in paper and mark them "危険" (dangerous))
- **Aluminium foil, lighters, umbrellas, disposable hand warmers (カイロ)** and so on

Notes:
- Put incandescent/LED bulbs in a paper bag marked "危険"
- Use up all the gas in lighters, and empty spray cans completely

---

## 3. Recyclables (資源) — Once a Week

Recyclables must be **bagged separately by type**; mixed bags are not collected.

![Shibuya's recycling guide, covering plastics, waste paper, bottles, cans, PET bottles and more](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10541034.png)
*Recycling is sorted in great detail, and each type is put out differently, which is easy to mix up when you first arrive*

The main categories:

**Plastics (プラスチック)**: cup noodle containers, shampoo bottles, CDs/DVDs and other plastics with the "プラ" mark. Rinse them with water, remove dirt, and put them in a see-through bag.

**Waste paper (古紙: newspapers, magazines, cardboard)**: tie newspapers, magazines and cardboard separately; magazines can go in a bag; flatten and tie cardboard boxes.

**Glass bottles (びん)**: only bottles for drinks, seasonings and other food (not cosmetics or medicine bottles). Rinse lightly and put in a see-through bag.

**Cans (缶)**: aluminium/steel drink cans. Rinse lightly and put in a see-through bag. Food tins (such as sardine tins with lingering smells) are sometimes not accepted, so check.

**PET bottles (ペットボトル)**: bottles with the "PET" mark. Remove the cap and label (the cap counts as plastic), rinse lightly and put in a see-through bag.

**Fluorescent tubes (circular, bulb-type, straight)**: certain types of fluorescent lights must go whole into the box they came in, wrapped in paper.

**Spray cans and gas canisters (スプレー缶・カセットボンベ)**: empty them completely and put them in a see-through bag. Never puncture them with an awl (dangerous!).

---

## 4. Oversized Rubbish (粗大ごみ) — Booking Required, Paid

Furniture, appliances and other large items (30cm or more) count as "oversized rubbish". **The ward does not collect it as normal**; you must **book in advance and pay**.

![Shibuya's guide to oversized rubbish and special waste](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10554886.png)
*Oversized rubbish needs a booking, and fees vary by item; large appliances (TVs, fridges, etc.) are covered separately by the Home Appliance Recycling Law*

How to book:
- **Online**: [https://p1.govtech-express.gov/web/]
- **Phone**: 03(3634)4777 (Monday to Friday, 9:00–17:00)
- **LINE**: search for "渋谷区粗大ごみ"

After booking, buy **oversized rubbish disposal tickets (stickers)** at a convenience store in the ward, stick them on the items, and put them at the designated spot by 8 a.m. on the scheduled day.

Large appliances (fridges, washing machines, TVs, air conditioners) are regulated by the **Home Appliance Recycling Law (家電リサイクル法)** and must be handled through the shop or a recycling company; the normal oversized rubbish process does not apply.

---

## 5. Collection Schedules by Area

In Shibuya, collection days differ by **neighbourhood block (町丁目)**. The same type of rubbish may be collected on completely different days on different streets.

![Shibuya's collection schedule by neighbourhood, showing collection days for burnable, non-burnable and recyclables](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10408621.png)
*Shibuya's collection schedule by neighbourhood block: always check the correct days for your own address*

When you move in, the management company usually gives you a rubbish guide; **I recommend saving it or taking a photo**, and adding the collection day for each type to your phone calendar. If you miss a collection day, you have to wait until the next week, and food waste piling up at home is quite unpleasant.

---

## Summary

Japan's rubbish sorting system looks tedious at first, but once you get used to it, there is a logic to it. A few key points to remember:

- Rubbish **must be put out by the designated time** at the designated spot
- Recyclables must be **bagged separately by type**; mixed bags are not collected
- Oversized rubbish needs **a booking in advance and a disposal ticket**
- **Collection days vary by street**, so check yours individually

It takes time to figure out at first, but it is also the first step to fitting into community life in Japan.

# 02 | Studying in Japan #2: Ward Office Move-in Registration, Health Insurance and National Pension

After entering Japan, the address field on your residence card still says "not yet determined". To make the card actually useful, the top priority is to go to your local **ward office (区役所)** and register your address, the "move-in procedure" (転入手続き).

## Move-in Registration at the Ward Office

In my case, my first home in Japan was a share house in Toshima ward, so I went to the **Toshima City Office** to register.

**Toshima City Office (豊島区役所)**
Address: 〒171-8422 2-45-1 Minami-Ikebukuro, Toshima-ku, Tokyo
Phone: 03-3981-1111　|　Hours: Monday to Friday 8:30–17:00

April is the peak season for new international students and new employees arriving in Japan, and ward offices everywhere have long queues. The waiting-time board at the entrance clearly showed that procedures such as "転入、転居、世帯の変更" (moving in, moving within the ward, household changes) had a wait of **over 180 minutes** that day, and that even after reaching the counter, the procedure itself would take another 120 minutes or more. Do not be discouraged when you see this; arriving early is the only solution.

To cut the wait, I chose to queue before opening: **I arrived at 6 a.m., and was served when the doors opened at 9**. If you do not want to spend the whole morning waiting, try to avoid peak times or allow plenty of time.

![Toshima ward office crowding notice: move-in waits of over 180 minutes](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10756712.png)
*The ward office in April, where waits are measured in hours*

Documents needed:
- **Passport**
- **Residence card** (already stamped by the immigration officer)
- **Proof of address** (your confirmed residential address)

At the counter, explain briefly in Japanese that you want to do "the procedure for moving into Toshima from overseas". The staff will give you a form; fill in the country you moved from (such as "中華民國" or "香港") and your new address.

![Toshima ward office move-in application form](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10684636.png)
*The resident change notification form (住民異動届出書): after filling it in, wait with your number ticket to be called*

After checking your details and residence card, if your address is complicated the staff may bring out a map and ask you to point to the exact building and room. Once confirmed, they record your address on the back of your residence card, and you can use the card for all later procedures.

Worth mentioning: if you do not have a Japanese phone number yet, just tell the staff "I don't have a phone number yet"; leaving that field blank does not stop the registration.

After the procedure, Toshima also gives you a "Welcome to Toshima City" guide for foreign residents, with an anime-style character on the cover, presumably to catch the eye of young international students.

![Toshima's guide booklet for new foreign residents, with an anime character on the cover](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10791594.png)
*Toshima's welcome booklet for foreign residents, with anime characters even on the cover: a thoughtful touch*

The whole move-in registration took about **an hour**.

## National Health Insurance

After move-in registration, the staff usually explain **National Health Insurance**. Once you have enrolled, the insurance card (or "eligibility certificate") arrives by post at your registered address about **two to three weeks later**, so keep an eye on your mailbox.

What I received was a "**国民健康保険資格確認書**" (National Health Insurance eligibility certificate), listing the insured person's name, date of birth, start date, co-payment ratio (usually 30%) and insurer number.

![Sample National Health Insurance eligibility certificate showing name, insurer number and co-payment ratio](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10641717.png)
*The National Health Insurance eligibility certificate: show it when seeing a doctor to confirm your insurance is valid*

## National Pension Exemption

International students at Japanese language schools usually have no income, so they can apply for a **full exemption from National Pension premiums**.

After move-in registration, go straight to the National Pension counter in the same building, tell the staff you are an international student with no current income, and apply for the exemption. The process is simple and needs no extra documents.

What happens after applying:
1. About two weeks later, you first receive a **payment notice** (even after applying for the exemption, a payment notice may still be sent).
2. About a month after that, you receive the **exemption notice** confirming the exemption is in effect.

The order of these two letters can be confusing, but there is no need to panic when the payment notice arrives; just wait for the official exemption letter.

## Tax Declaration

If you entered Japan **before April** (that is, you were resident in Japan during that tax year), you may also need to file a tax declaration. Since international students usually have no income in Japan, just enter "zero" in the income field; it is fairly simple.

## Summary

The whole ward office process — move-in registration, the National Health Insurance explanation, the National Pension exemption application and even the tax declaration — **takes about two to three hours in total**, and can basically all be done in one morning.

I recommend doing it **as soon as possible** after arrival, because only once the address on your residence card is confirmed can you move on to the phone number, bank account and later steps.

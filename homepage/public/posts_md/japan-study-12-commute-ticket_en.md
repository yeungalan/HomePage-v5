# 12 | Studying in Japan #12: Buying a PASMO Student Commuter Pass — From the Form to the Card

Almost everyone who commutes to work or school in Tokyo has a **PASMO** or **Suica** card. And for anyone travelling to school or work every day, the thing most worth getting is a "**commuter pass (定期券, teikiken)**".

This post records how I bought a PASMO student commuter pass on the Toei Subway, including the difference between PASMO and Suica, how to fill in the application form, the commuting certificate issued by the school, and how to read the details printed on the pass.

---

## 1. What Is a Commuter Pass?

The biggest benefit of a commuter pass is that, within its validity period, you can **travel between point A and point B as many times as you like**. Usually A and B are the station near your home and the station near your workplace or school.

Besides saving on daily fares, a commuter pass has an often-overlooked benefit: **you can get on and off freely at any station in between** at no extra cost. On my route, the Toei Shinjuku Line from Shinjuku to Jimbocho passes through Shinjuku-sanchome, Ichigaya, Kudanshita and other stations, all covered by the pass.

So if your commute happens to pass busy areas such as Tokyo, Shinjuku, Ikebukuro, Shibuya or Shinagawa, you can shop or wander after class or work without paying extra fares, which adds up to quite a saving over time.

---

## 2. What Is the Difference Between PASMO and Suica?

Many people new to Tokyo ask: which should I get, PASMO or Suica?

In short, both are top-up **transport IC cards** for riding trains and buses and paying at convenience stores and vending machines. Since the nationwide transport IC cards became interoperable, there is almost no difference between them for everyday rides and shopping.

The real differences are mainly the **issuer** and **which commuter passes you can buy**:

| Item | Suica | PASMO |
|------|-------|-------|
| Issuer | JR East | Tokyo Metro, Toei Subway, Tokyu, Odakyu and other Kanto private railways, subways and bus companies |
| Commuter passes available | JR line passes | Subway, private railway and bus passes |
| Points | JRE POINT (separate registration needed) | No common points scheme; each company runs its own (e.g. Tokyo Metro's "メトロポイントPlus") |
| Deposit (デポジット) | 500 yen | 500 yen |
| Mobile version | Mobile Suica (iPhone / Android) | Mobile PASMO (iPhone / Android) |
| Rides and shopping | Interoperable with transport IC cards nationwide | Interoperable with transport IC cards nationwide |

So which card to choose comes down to **which company runs your commuter pass route**:

- If your commute is mainly on **JR lines** → choose **Suica**
- If your commute is mainly on **subways or private railways** → choose **PASMO**

My departure and arrival stations are both on the **Toei Subway**, one of PASMO's issuers, so buying a pass at a Toei station naturally gave me a PASMO.

> <span data-icon="mingcute:bulb-line"></span> One PASMO can carry at most one rail commuter pass and one bus commuter pass. If your route crosses companies, ask at the counter whether you can get a "連絡定期券" (through pass), so one card covers the whole route.

## 3. Documents Needed

To buy a **student commuter pass** for the first time, you must go in person to a station's **commuter pass office (定期券うりば)**; you cannot buy it directly from a ticket machine. Bring:

- **Commuter pass application form (定期券購入申込書)** (pick it up at the office and fill it in on the spot)
- **Commuting certificate (通学証明書)** (issued by your school)
- **Student ID**
- **Payment**: cash or credit card

> <span data-icon="mingcute:warning-line"></span> The commuting certificate is valid for only **one month**, so go as soon as possible after the school issues it. Toei Subway passes can be bought up to **14 days before the start date**, so you can plan ahead.

---

## 4. Filling in the Application Form

At the commuter pass office, ask the staff for an application form. It has two parts: the top half is the personal details for the PASMO card, and the bottom half, "定期券内容" (pass details), is the details of the pass.

![PASMO commuter pass application form with "大人用 PASMO 定期券の購入", "新規", "通学（その他）" and "3ヶ月" circled, start date 2 May 2026](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/IMG_4839.JPEG)
*The commuter pass application form: the top half is the PASMO personal details, and the bottom half, "定期券内容", only needs filling in if you are buying a pass*

### Top half: personal details

- **お申込み内容 (what you are applying for)**: circle "**大人用 PASMO 定期券の購入**" (adult PASMO commuter pass purchase).
- **オナマエ (name)**: write your full name in katakana or Latin letters, starting from the leftmost box, one character per box, with a space between family and given names; marks such as the dakuten (゛) also take a box. Foreigners are best off writing it in English, matching the spelling on their residence card.
- **生年月日 (date of birth)**: first circle "**西暦**" (Western calendar), then fill in the year, month and day.
- **電話番号 (phone number)**: start from the leftmost box; a mobile number is fine.
- **PASMO 購入金額 (PASMO purchase amount)**: the amount to load onto the card in advance, in units of 1,000 yen up to 20,000 yen; the form notes this includes the 500 yen deposit. If you are only buying a pass and do not want to top up for now, you can write "0" like I did.
- **性別 (sex)**: circle "男" (male) or "女" (female).

### Bottom half: pass details

The middle of the form says "※以下は、定期券が必要なお客さまのみ必要事項をご記入ください", meaning the section below is only for those buying a commuter pass.

- **お申し込みの内容 (application type)**: circle "**新規**" (new) for a first purchase; "継続" (renewal) to renew; "PASMO定期券への変更" to switch from an old magnetic pass to PASMO.
- **種別 (type)**: if you only ride within the Toei Subway, circle "**地下鉄**" (subway). If you need to transfer to the Toden streetcar, Toei buses or the Nippori-Toneri Liner, choose the matching "連絡" (through) option; "全線（都営地下鉄）" is a pass for unlimited rides on the whole Toei Subway.
- **種類 (kind)**: circle "通勤" for work or "**通学**" for school. After choosing 通学, also circle the school category on the right; language school students circle "**その他**" (other).
- **乗車区間 (route)**: write the start and end stations. I wrote "**新宿 〜 神保町**" (Shinjuku – Jimbocho). If you transfer on the way, write the stations or lines you pass through in the "経由" (via) field; for a direct route you can leave it blank.
- **使用開始日 (start date)**: the date the pass takes effect. I wrote **2 May 2026**.
- **有効期間 (validity period)**: 1, 3 or 6 months; the longer the period, the better the monthly value. My first time I chose "**3ヶ月**" (3 months).
- **お支払い方法 (payment method)**: cash or credit card; I paid by **credit card**.
- **住所（自宅）及学校名 (home address and school name)**: required for student passes; write your address and school name.

The section at the very bottom, "下欄には記入しないでください" (do not write below), is for station staff, so ignore it.

> <span data-icon="mingcute:warning-line"></span> Two notes on the right of the form are worth knowing: the registered PASMO and the commuter pass must be in the same person's name, or you cannot buy it; and a registered PASMO without a commuter pass cannot be bought by credit card.

## 5. The Commuting Certificate from the School

To buy a student commuter pass, besides the application form you must also submit a **commuting certificate (通学証明書) issued by your school**. You need to request it from the school office in advance. It lists your name, address, course and year, student ID number, commuting route, and the pass's validity period and start date.

![Commuting certificate issued by the Kudan Institute of Japanese Language & Culture, with the school's seal and "一般日本語コース" in the course and year field](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/IMG_4840.JPEG)
*The commuting certificate issued by the school: everything except the "※" field is filled in by the school and stamped with its official seal, and it is valid for one month from the issue date*

The notes at the bottom of the certificate set out the rules: apart from the fields marked "※", which the student fills in, everything is filled in by the school. The fields are:

- **通学者の氏名・年齢及び性別**: the student's name, age and sex (sex is circled).
- **通学者の居住地**: the student's address.
- **学科及び学年**: the course and year, e.g. mine says "一般 日本語コース" (general Japanese course).
- **身分証明書番号**: student ID number.
- **通学区間**: the commuting route, i.e. Shinjuku to Jimbocho.
- **通学定期乗車券の有効期間**: the validity period of the pass.
- **※通学定期乗車券の使用開始日**: the only field the student fills in; write the same start date as on the application form.
- **Issue date, school address, school name and school representative's name**: filled in by the school and stamped with the school's **official seal (職印)**.

A few points to note:

- The certificate is valid for **one month from the issue date** (including the issue date); once it expires you have to request a new one.
- Any correction to the "※" field must be stamped with the student's own **personal seal (認印)**, and corrections to other fields need the school representative's **official seal**, or the certificate is invalid. (See #5 for the types of seals.)
- The section at the very bottom, "下欄には記入しないでください", is filled in by station staff.

---

## 6. Submitting the Documents and Collecting the Pass

Once the application form is filled in, hand the **application form, commuting certificate and student ID** to the staff at the commuter pass office. They check that the details match across all three; once confirmed, they take payment and issue a PASMO card with the pass details printed on it on the spot.

When you get the card, check on the spot that the **route, start date and expiry date** on the card are correct. Once everything is fine, on the start date you can simply tap through the gates.

---

## 7. How to Read the Details on a PASMO Commuter Pass

The pass details are printed directly on the front of the PASMO card. Here is an explanation using a sample pass from the Tokyo Metropolitan Bureau of Transportation:

![Sample Tokyo Metropolitan Bureau of Transportation PASMO commuter pass, printed with the route 都庁前 ↔ 銀座, the via route, expiry date and holder's name](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-8-23179747.png)
*A sample PASMO commuter pass: the biggest line is the expiry date, with the route and via lines printed above it*

- **Top left, "通勤1箇月"**: the type of pass (work or school) and validity period.
- **Top middle, "（東京都交通局）"**: the company that sold the pass.
- **Top right, "No.00000"**: the pass number.
- **Large text in the middle, "都庁前 ↔ 銀座"**: the route, i.e. the two stations you can travel between as much as you like.
- **"経由：内回・六本木・日比線"**: the via route. In this example, you ride the Oedo Line (inner loop) from Tochomae to Roppongi, then change to the Hibiya Line for Ginza.
- **Right side, "0月 0日から"**: the start date.
- **The largest text, "0000.00.00 まで"**: the expiry date, the last day the pass can be used, and the line to watch most.
- **Bottom left, "00000円"**: the price of the pass.
- **"パスモ タロウ 様"**: the holder's name, printed in katakana or Latin letters.
- **Bottom right, the date and "馬喰横山駅 1発行"**: the date and station of sale.

> <span data-icon="mingcute:bulb-line"></span> After the pass expires, the PASMO card itself can still be used as an ordinary IC card, and the balance on it is unaffected. To renew, as long as it is within the same school year and the route is unchanged, you can usually do it at a station's commuter pass machine; but a first student pass, or renewing across school years, must be done at the counter.

## Summary

Buying a student commuter pass is not complicated; with your documents ready, it can be done in one visit to the counter. Key points:

- Whether to choose **PASMO** or **Suica** depends on whether your route is on a **subway, private railway or JR**
- A first student commuter pass must be bought at the **commuter pass office (定期券うりば)**
- Bring the **application form, commuting certificate and student ID**
- The commuting certificate is valid for only **one month**, so go soon after getting it
- Passes can be bought up to **14 days before** the start date
- You can **get on and off freely** at stations along the route; making good use of this saves a lot on fares

With the pass in hand, the daily trip to school also comes with a bit more freedom to explore Tokyo along the way.

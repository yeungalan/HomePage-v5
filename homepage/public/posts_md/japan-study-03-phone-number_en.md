# 03 | Studying in Japan #3: Getting a Phone Number in Japan

Having a local Japanese phone number is an almost essential step for life in Japan. Many services, including opening a bank account, signing up for PayPay and all kinds of online registration, need a Japanese mobile number for verification. Getting a number as early as possible is an important part of settling in.

## Choosing a Plan

The common choices for international students right now are mainly:

- **Rakuten Mobile (楽天モバイル)**: cheaper monthly fees, and it has physical shops, which makes signing up fairly convenient.
- **LINE Mobile (LINEモバイル)**: also a low-cost plan, but only available online, with no physical shops.

Rakuten Mobile's pricing is very straightforward: you pay by monthly usage, 980 yen up to 3 GB, 1,980 yen up to 20 GB, and capped at 2,980 yen above 20 GB (all prices before tax), plus unlimited free domestic calls through its app. For students whose usage varies, this flexible pricing works well.

![Rakuten Mobile's plan pricing table on its website](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10529353.png)
*Rakuten Mobile's "最強プラン": billed automatically by actual usage, with a monthly cap of 2,980 yen*

I first considered LINE Mobile, but its website would not let me complete the online application, and since LINE Mobile has no physical shop to visit, I ended up choosing **Rakuten Mobile**.

## Signing Up

I went to the **Rakuten Mobile shop in Shinjuku**. Inside, I told the staff I wanted a phone number, and they handed me a stack of confirmation documents and explained the contract item by item.

You only need basic Japanese for the whole process, enough to follow simple questions and answers. One important detail to confirm is whether your phone uses a **physical SIM card** or an **eSIM**:

- Newer iPhones usually use an **eSIM**
- Most Android phones use a **physical SIM card**

Once that is clear, the staff issue a SIM in the matching format.

You also need to **link a credit card** for the monthly bill. I recommend having an international credit card that works in Japan ready in advance to make the process smoother.

## Actual Usage

About a month after activating the number, the Rakuten Mobile app showed my actual usage and charges clearly. For the whole of April I used about **11.69 GB** of data, which falls in the up-to-20 GB tier, and the bill for the month was **2,412 yen** (including tax).

![Rakuten Mobile app showing April usage of 11.69 GB and a monthly total of 2,412 yen](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10611133.png)
*Rakuten Mobile's usage dashboard: data usage is shown in real time and charges are calculated automatically from usage, so there is no need to worry about overage fees*

For a student who uses social media, map navigation and music streaming every day, that usage and cost is quite reasonable.

## Next Steps After Getting a Number

With a Japanese phone number, you can start signing up for all kinds of services that need number verification, such as:

- **PayPay** (the most widely used mobile payment in Japan)
- **Bank accounts** (especially online banks that require phone verification)
- Accounts on various online platforms

You could say a phone number is a key to life in Japan, opening the door to a whole series of later procedures. I recommend getting a number at a carrier's shop as soon as you have finished move-in registration at the ward office, so the rest of settling in can go smoothly.

# 11 | Studying in Japan #11: First Term Done — Grades and Reflections

Before I knew it, a whole term had gone by.

When I first came to Japan, my Japanese was in an awkward place. I had studied for the JLPT and had some grammar foundation, but the moment a real person spoke to me, my brain froze. I could not understand what they were saying, and I did not know how to respond. The gap between textbook Japanese and real-life Japanese was much wider than I had expected.

But as time went on, things slowly started to change. What I picked up in class, the vocabulary I drilled every day and the Japanese input everywhere in daily life slowly stacked up and began to take effect. I went from not understanding a word the shop staff said to communicating in simple Japanese and trying to sort things out in Japanese at the convenience store. The change is small, but it is real.

What surprised me most was the speed. A few months of immersion in Japan felt like it took me further than several years of self-study at home. "Immersion" is not just a piece of study advice; it is the core method itself.

Honestly, I had prepared myself for something around 60 to 70. I know my foundation still has plenty of gaps, and I was not very confident about several of the listening questions. Getting full marks in listening was unexpected, but a good surprise.

## What Helped Me Improve

A large part of it is thanks to the people around me.

![Last day of class, a photo with my classmates](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-5-22038652.png)
*Classmates from different countries: all at a similar level, making mistakes and improving together with a laugh*

My classmates come from all over the world, and everyone was willing to patiently stumble through conversations with me in broken Japanese. Learning among people at a similar level, who are also making mistakes and also laughing and trying again, feels uniquely comfortable.

The teachers were just as patient. Whenever I did not get a grammar point, they would sit down and explain it again carefully, and never made me feel I was falling behind.

## What I Still Need to Work On

The thing I am least confident about is still vocabulary. Some days I just cannot bring myself to drill words, and as soon as I slack off, the gaps show when I speak. I roughly know what I want to say, but get stuck because one word will not come to mind, which is quite frustrating. That is the main thing I want to fix next term.

Plans for next term: consolidate my grammar foundation, strengthen vocabulary, and use Japanese as much as possible outside class.

Goal for the end of the year: JLPT N2.

# 13 | Studying in Japan #13: National Pension Premium Exemption — The General Exemption for Language School Students

In #2 I briefly mentioned that after registering your move-in at the ward office, you can apply for an exemption from National Pension premiums at the same time. This step may seem minor, but it is an important procedure everyone arriving in Japan should handle early, especially international students.

This post records the full application process in detail, including why language school students do not qualify for the usual "student payment deferral", what documents arrive after applying, and how to read the exemption approval notice that comes at the end.

---

## 1. Why Apply for a Pension Exemption?

In principle, everyone with an address in Japan aged 20 to 59 must join the **National Pension (国民年金)**, regardless of nationality, and international students are no exception.

National Pension premiums are not cheap: in fiscal Reiwa 8 (2026) they are **17,920 yen** a month. If you do nothing, you have to pay this every month, which comes to over 210,000 yen a year.

For students with no income, once an exemption is applied for and approved, **you do not have to pay** the premiums for that period. So this is one of the procedures to handle as early as possible after arriving in Japan.

---

## 2. Student Payment Deferral versus the General Exemption

International students usually have two routes to a pension exemption:

- **Student payment deferral (学生納付特例)**: for students at universities, junior colleges, vocational schools and similar institutions; you can apply directly at the ward office.
- **General exemption (申請免除)**: for people with low income who have difficulty paying premiums.

Most international students use the **student payment deferral**. However, not every school qualifies; for example, miscellaneous schools (各種学校) must offer a course of **at least one year** to be eligible. My language school **does not qualify for the student payment deferral**, so I had to apply for the **general exemption** instead.

| Item | Student payment deferral | General exemption |
|------|------------|---------|
| Who it covers | Students at universities, junior colleges, vocational schools, etc. (miscellaneous schools must have courses of at least one year) | People with low income who have difficulty paying (including students not covered by the student deferral) |
| Whose income is assessed | The student only | The applicant, spouse and head of household |
| Period covered | April to March of the following year | July to June of the following year |
| Effect on future pension | Not counted towards the pension amount | Full exemption counts as 4/8 |

> <span data-icon="mingcute:bulb-line"></span> If a student qualifies for the student payment deferral, they must apply for that first and cannot choose the general exemption. To check whether your school qualifies, see the Japan Pension Service's "list of schools covered by the student payment deferral" or ask the ward office staff.

---

## 3. Application Process

Applying for the general exemption is actually quite simple, and the ward office staff can help you through the whole thing.

### Step 1: Go to the ward office's National Pension counter

Go to the **National Pension counter at your local ward office** and tell the staff you want to apply for an exemption from National Pension premiums. If you have just registered your move-in, you can do it the same day.

### Step 2: Staff confirm your details

The staff ask a few basic questions to check whether you are eligible, such as:

- Whether you are currently a student
- Which school you attend
- Your current income

### Step 3: Fill in the application form

Once your details are confirmed, the staff help you fill in the "**国民年金保険料免除・納付猶予申請書**" (application for exemption from or deferral of National Pension premiums). There are four kinds of premium exemption:

- **Full exemption**
- **3/4 exemption**
- **Half exemption**
- **1/4 exemption**

Usually you apply for all four together. They are reviewed in your preferred order, so even if a full exemption is not approved, you may still get a partial one. There is also a "**payment deferral (納付猶予)**" system for people aged under 50, which you can apply for at the same time.

### Step 4: The ward office submits it to the Japan Pension Service

Once the form is complete, the ward office staff submit it on your behalf to the **Japan Pension Service (日本年金機構)**, which reviews whether you meet the exemption criteria. After that, you just wait at home for the result.

---

## 4. Waiting for the Review: Do Not Panic About Payment Slips

About **a month** after applying, a thick bundle of National Pension premium **payment slips (納付書)** arrives at home.

Many people panic when they receive these, thinking their application failed, but this is normal. The ward office staff specifically warned me: **if you receive payment slips during the review, do not worry; you do not need to pay before the official result comes out.** You can put the bundle aside and ignore it for now.

About half a month later, roughly **six weeks** after applying, another postcard arrives: the "**国民年金保険料免除・納付猶予申請承認通知書**" (notice of approval of the application for exemption from or deferral of National Pension premiums).

---

## 5. How to Read the Exemption Approval Notice

Open the notice: the left page shows the review result, and the right page explains the notice.

![National Pension premium exemption/deferral approval notice; the left page lists the review order and "全額免除 令和8年4月分～令和8年6月分", the right page explains the notice and shows the pension amounts for each exemption type](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/IMG_6228.JPEG)
*The exemption approval notice: the most important thing on the left page is the type and period of exemption approved, and the right page explains how each type of exemption affects your future pension*

### Left page: the review result

- **基礎年金番号 (basic pension number)**: your basic pension number, needed for future pension procedures, so keep it safe.
- **令和 8 年 4 月 27 日**: the date the notice was issued (Reiwa 8 is 2026).
- **審査区分 (review order)**: the order in which you asked for each type to be reviewed. On my notice, full exemption was 1, payment deferral 2, 3/4 exemption 3, half exemption 4 and 1/4 exemption 5; types not applied for show "―".
- **承認結果 (approval result)**: the most important line on the whole notice. Mine says "**全額免除　令和 8 年 4 月分 ～ 令和 8 年 6 月分**", i.e. a full exemption of premiums from April to June 2026.
- **継続審査申出受付済**: means a request for "continuous review" has been accepted (see below).
- **Small print at the bottom**: if you disagree with the result, you can request a review by a social insurance examiner within three months from the day after you learn of the decision.

You may be wondering: why only until June? Because the general exemption is reviewed on a year running **from July to June of the following year**. I applied in April, so my first approval period only ran until June of that year.

### Right page: explanation of the notice

The top half of the right page explains three notes that may appear on the notice:

- **"期間延長承認" (extension approved)**: you had applied in the previous year for "full exemption or payment deferral to be reviewed in following years too", so approval continues.
- **"今後、免除申請が必要です。" (you need to apply for an exemption from now on)**: payment deferral only applies while you are under 50; after that you need to pay premiums or apply for an exemption again.
- **"継続審査申出受付済" (continuous review request accepted)**: from now on the Japan Pension Service will **automatically** review for full exemption or payment deferral, so **you do not have to reapply every year**. However, if your spouse situation changes through marriage, divorce and so on, you must submit a notice of change within 14 days.

My notice says "継続審査申出受付済", so from July onwards each year is reviewed automatically, saving me a trip to the ward office every year.

The bottom half of the right page explains how exemptions affect your **future pension amount**. Exempt periods count towards the qualifying period for receiving a pension, but the amount is lower than if you had paid in full:

| Exemption type | Future pension amount (full payment = 1) |
|---------|-------------------------|
| Full payment | 1 |
| Full exemption | 4/8 |
| 3/4 exemption | 5/8 |
| Half exemption | 6/8 |
| 1/4 exemption | 7/8 |
| Payment deferral | 0 (not counted towards the pension amount) |

> <span data-icon="mingcute:warning-line"></span> If what is approved is a **partial exemption** (3/4, half or 1/4), you still have to pay the remaining part of the premium. If you do not, that period is treated as **unpaid (未納)** and counts towards neither the pension amount nor the qualifying period.

The bottom of the right page also points to "ways to increase your pension amount" on the back. One of these is **back payment (追納)**: for approved exemption periods, you can pay the premiums later **within 10 years**. If you plan to stay and work in Japan, you can consider back payment later.

---

## Summary

The whole general exemption process, from applying at the ward office to receiving the approval notice, took about six weeks. As long as the notice says "**全額免除**" (full exemption), you do not need to pay premiums for the approved period. Key points:

- **Apply for a pension exemption as soon as possible** after arriving in Japan; it saves 17,920 yen a month (fiscal Reiwa 8)
- Eligible students must **apply for the student payment deferral first**; language school students who do not qualify apply for the **general exemption**
- There are four kinds of exemption — **full, 3/4, half and 1/4** — usually applied for together
- **Do not panic about payment slips** during the review; you do not need to pay before the official result
- The general exemption runs on a **July-to-June** year; once **continuous review** is accepted, you do not need to reapply every year
- If you only get a **partial exemption**, remember to pay the rest, or it will be treated as unpaid

Also, if you start working part-time later (see #7 on permission to work), your higher income may change the next year's review result, so keep an eye on what the notice says.

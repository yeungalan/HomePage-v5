# 15 | Studying in Japan #15: Reflections on Half a Year in Japan — The Last Hundred Days of 2026

Time really flies: in the blink of an eye there are fewer than a hundred days left in 2026. Thinking back to arriving in Japan in April and starting life here, it has now been about half a year. It is time to stop and properly look back on how this half-year has felt.

In this half-year I have done a lot, and I feel I have grown quite a bit.

## The First Three Months: Learning to Handle Everything on My Own

My first three months in Japan were mostly spent on documents and procedures: the residence card, move-in registration at the ward office, a phone number, a bank account, the pension exemption, the My Number Card... The earlier posts in this series record exactly this period.

None of these procedures is especially difficult on its own, but in a country whose language I had not yet mastered, I had to look everything up myself, go to every counter myself, and work out every solution myself. For me, the biggest gain of these three months was not how many documents I got done, but learning to **handle everything in my life independently**.

## After Three Months: Taking On More New Things

After three months, life gradually settled into a routine, and I started to have the energy to take on more and different things.

In June, I took my first domestic flight in Japan, to **Tanegashima** in Kagoshima, and visited the rocket launch site at the **Tanegashima Space Center**, a trip I had been planning for years. On the same trip, I also made a pilgrimage to the real-life locations of *5 Centimeters per Second* (秒速5センチメートル), whose second episode is set on Tanegashima. Scenery I had only seen on screen for years, I finally saw with my own eyes.

Later, during the Mount Fuji climbing season, I climbed **Mount Fuji** and watched the sunrise from the mountain. I also went to a **fireworks festival** with a big group of friends, and took plenty of short trips around Tokyo, slowly discovering different corners of the city.

## Japanese: From Not Saying a Word to Starting to Communicate

Add the Japanese I have learned at school, and I can now say a few things in Japanese in most of the most basic situations of daily life in Japan.

Thinking back to when I first arrived, I could barely get a word out; now I can say a bit more in Japanese, and I am better able to deal with all sorts of problems than before. The change has built up bit by bit, but looking back, it is quite noticeable.

## Finishing an Exam I Had Always Wanted to Take

Just a few weeks ago I finished an exam. It is one I had always wanted to take when I was working, but could never find the time to prepare for. After coming to Japan I finally had time to prepare properly, and now that it is done, I think I actually did pretty well.

## Still Room to Improve

Of course, not every aspect of this half-year went well. Last term I did not study very seriously, and my end-of-term result was only just a pass. That is something I think I must improve, and I hope to study harder in the upcoming **N3 course**.

I feel that in these few months I may have done more than many people do in several years. At the same time, I know there is still plenty of room for me to improve. I want to keep putting in more time to try new things, explore new places, and make progress in Japanese. Only then can I pass the N3 exam that follows and the **JLPT** at the end of the year.

## Goals for the Last Hundred Days of 2026

For the rest of 2026, there are five things I am learning and hope to keep improving:

1. **Japanese**: study seriously in the N3 course and aim to pass the N3 exam and the year-end JLPT.
2. **Exploring and trying**: keep going to more new places and trying more new things.
3. **Skiing**: keep learning to ski in December, and hopefully really learn how this season.
4. **Cooking**: my cooking is still not great; I hope to learn to make tastier dishes rather than staying with the simplest ones.
5. **Cleaning**: cleaning the flat once a week is still a bit of a chore for me. I am thinking about ways to make cleaning easier.

These five are all things I am still learning. I hope to keep improving myself in the last hundred days of 2026, and then welcome a better 2027.

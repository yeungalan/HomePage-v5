# 04 | Studying in Japan #4: Opening a Bank Account in Japan

For students who have just arrived in Japan, opening a bank account is an unavoidable hurdle. The Japanese banking system is fairly conservative; in the past many banks required you to visit in person and sometimes refused foreigners on grounds such as "period of stay too short" or "no history in Japan". Today, however, **Japan Post Bank (ゆうちょ銀行)** lets you apply through its smartphone app, greatly lowering the barrier for students.

## Why Japan Post Bank

For newly arrived students, Japan Post Bank is almost certainly **the easiest first bank account to open**, because:

- You can apply online through the smartphone app, with no need to queue at a counter
- Its review is relatively lenient for international students
- Japan Post Bank ATMs are everywhere in Japan, including convenience stores and post offices, so deposits and withdrawals are very convenient

## Opening an Account Online with the ゆうちょ手続きアプリ

Japan Post Bank's official "**ゆうちょ手続きアプリ**" (procedures app) lets you open an account entirely on your phone, without visiting a counter.

- <span data-icon="mingcute:link-line"></span> [ゆうちょ手続きアプリ official page](https://www.jp-bank.japanpost.jp/app/tetsuzuki/app_tz_en.html)
- <span data-icon="mingcute:file-line"></span> [ゆうちょ手続きアプリ quick guide (PDF)](https://www.jp-bank.japanpost.jp/app/tetsuzuki/pdf/guide_jp_quick.pdf)

**Application steps (in brief):**

1. Download the ゆうちょ手続きアプリ, agree to the terms of use, and on the home screen choose "**新しく口座をつくりたい**" (open a new account).
2. Choose your **nationality** and verify your identity with an ID document (you need either a driver's licence or a My Number Card; if you hold a residence card, I recommend using a My Number Card with instant eKYC verification).
3. Follow the in-app prompts to enter your details (name, address, purpose of the account, etc.) and set these passwords:
   - **Cash card PIN** (暗証番号, 4 digits)
   - **ゆうちょダイレクト login password** (8–12 characters, including upper-case letters, lower-case letters and numbers)
4. Confirm and submit. If the instant review passes, your **cash card (キャッシュカード)** is sent to your registered address by registered mail **within about a week**.

> <span data-icon="mingcute:warning-line"></span> Accounts opened in the app are **passbook-free (ゆうちょダイレクト+)**, i.e. they come without a passbook. If you need one, after receiving your cash card go to any ATM that supports passbook renewal to have a passbook issued (free the first time, within 2 months of opening the account).

## Getting a Passbook (通帳)

As mentioned, app-opened accounts come without a passbook by default, but many institutions in Japan still ask for one as proof, so I recommend getting one at an ATM.

The steps:

**Step 1**: on the ATM main menu, choose "通帳記入・通帳繰越　有通帳口座への切替" (passbook update / switch to a passbook account).

![The Japan Post Bank ATM main menu](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10946656.png)
*The Japan Post Bank ATM main menu: there is an "English Guide" button at the bottom right, so you can use it without Japanese*

**Step 2**: the ATM asks you to choose between "通帳とカード" (passbook and card) and "カードのみ" (card only). Choose "通帳とカード" to enable the passbook.

![ATM asking you to choose "通帳とカード" or "カードのみ"](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10563134.png)
*Choose "通帳とカード" so the account supports both a physical passbook and a cash card*

**Step 3**: follow the ATM's instructions and confirm issuing a new passbook.

![ATM prompting you to insert the passbook](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10951198.png)
*The ATM prompts for the passbook; on first issue it prints and dispenses the passbook automatically*

**Step 4**: the ATM dispenses the passbook, and you are done.

![The ATM's passbook slot dispensing a newly issued passbook](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10960222.png)
*The passbook comes out of the ATM's "通帳 PASSBOOK" slot; it takes just a few minutes*

The passbook has the signature green cover, with "総合口座通帳" printed on the front.

![The green cover of a Japan Post Bank general account passbook](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10692594.png)
*Japan Post Bank's "総合口座通帳": it looks old-fashioned, but it is indispensable for life in Japan*

Once done, you can deposit money at the same ATM; when the balance shows correctly, your bank account is officially up and running.

## Forgot Your PIN? How to Reset It

If you forget your cash card PIN, you can reset it online through the ゆうちょ手続きアプリ without going to a counter:

1. Open the app and on the home screen choose "**暗証番号を再登録したい**" (re-register PIN).
2. Enter your account's **記号番号** (a 5-digit symbol plus a number of up to 8 digits, shown on your passbook or cash card).
3. Choose how to verify your identity: the **ゆうちょ認証アプリ** or an **ID document** (driver's licence / My Number Card).
4. After verification, enter a new 4-digit PIN and the reset is complete.

> <span data-icon="mingcute:warning-line"></span> If your passbook is also locked because of wrong PIN entries, the app reset only unlocks the cash card; to unlock the passbook you need to visit a post office or Japan Post Bank counter.

## Other Banks

Japan Post Bank is the best choice for a first account, but as you build up a history of living in Japan you can consider accounts at other banks, such as **PayPay Bank** or **SBI Sumishin Net Bank (住信SBIネット銀行)**, which are more flexible for e-payments and transfers. Once you have a Japanese phone number, these banks can also be opened online.

## Summary

Opening a bank account in Japan used to be a big problem for foreigners, but applying through Japan Post Bank's app has simplified the process a lot. I recommend applying as soon as you have a phone number, because a bank account is the basis for many essential services later on, such as automatic rent payments, salary deposits and regular bills.

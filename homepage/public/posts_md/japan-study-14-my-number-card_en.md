# 14 | Studying in Japan #14: Applying for a My Number Card — From the Notification to Collecting the Card

Living in Japan, besides your residence card, another very important card is the "**My Number Card (マイナンバーカード)**". It makes life in Japan a lot simpler afterwards, and many services need it.

Since it takes about **six weeks** from receiving the application form to actually getting the card, I recommend applying as soon as you have registered your move-in. This post records the full process of applying for a My Number Card, and how to read and fill in each document.

---

## 1. What Is the My Number Card For?

Everyone who completes resident registration in Japan, regardless of nationality, is assigned a 12-digit "**personal number (マイナンバー, My Number)**". The My Number Card is the physical card printed with this number, with your photo and an IC chip.

With this card, many procedures become much more convenient, for example:

- **Opening a bank account**: it works as ID, and some bank apps can read the card's chip directly to verify you online (see #4 on opening a Japan Post Bank account)
- **Printing residence certificates at convenience stores**: no more trips to the ward office; print your residence certificate on a convenience store's multifunction machine
- **Using it as your health insurance card (マイナ保険証)**
- **Applying for online services**: many online services ask for your My Number Card and personal number when you sign up; even lotteries such as those for Pokémon cards (ポケモンカード) sometimes require identity verification with a My Number Card

> <span data-icon="mingcute:bulb-line"></span> For foreign residents, the My Number Card's expiry date is tied to the **expiry date of the period of stay** on your residence card. After renewing your period of stay, remember to also update your My Number Card's expiry at the ward office.

---

## 2. Receiving the Personal Number Notification

First, when you register your move-in at the ward office, tell the staff you want to apply for a My Number Card. After resident registration, the ward office sends a letter to your address.

> <span data-icon="mingcute:bulb-line"></span> The personal number is assigned automatically after resident registration, so the letter usually arrives even if you do not specifically ask.

The envelope mainly contains two sheets: the first is the "**personal number notification (個人番号通知書)**" and the second is the "**My Number Card application form (個人番号カード交付申請書)**", along with a return envelope and an explanatory booklet.

![Personal number notification issued by Toshima ward; the top half lists the personal number, name and date of birth, the bottom half explains how to apply for a My Number Card](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/IMG_6229.JPEG)
*The personal number notification: the top half shows your 12-digit personal number. This sheet is never reissued, so keep it safe*

The notification contains the following:

- **Top right, "令和 8 年 4 月 7 日発行　豊島区長"**: the issue date and issuing ward office (I moved to Shibuya after receiving the notification, so the later collection notice came from Shibuya).
- **Personal number, name, date of birth**: your 12-digit personal number, name and date of birth.
- **First "○"**: this notification tells you your personal number.
- **Second "○"**: this notification **cannot** be used as "a document proving your personal number" or "ID". When you need to prove your personal number, show your My Number Card or submit a residence certificate that includes the number.
- **Third "○"**: "本通知書の再発行は行いません", i.e. **it is not reissued if lost**. This is very important; be sure to keep it safe.
- **"マイナンバーカードの申請について" (about applying for the card)**: explains the two ways to apply: online by scanning the QR code on the right with your phone, or by post with the enclosed envelope and application form.
- **Application form ID (申請書 ID)**: the number you need to check on the progress of your application.
- **Enquiry number at the bottom**: the My Number toll-free line 0120-95-0178 (weekdays 9:30–20:00, weekends and holidays 9:30–17:30).

---

## 3. Applying for the My Number Card

There are two ways to apply: by post and online.

### Method 1: By post

The second sheet is the "**個人番号カード交付申請書 兼 電子証明書発行／更新申請書**" (application for issue of a personal number card and issue/renewal of electronic certificates).

![My Number Card application form; the photo box is at the top right, and the right side shows the foreign resident category as mid- to long-term resident](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/IMG_6230.JPEG)
*The My Number Card application form: name, address and other details are pre-printed; you only need to attach a photo and fill in your phone number, the application date and your name*

Most of the form is pre-printed from your residence record, so there is actually not much to do yourself:

- **氏名、住所、生年月日、性別 (name, address, date of birth, sex)**: pre-printed; just check they are correct. The form notes that these are printed on the card exactly as recorded on your residence record.
- **外国人住民の区分・在留期間等満了日 (foreign resident category and expiry of period of stay)**: also pre-printed, e.g. mine says "中長期在留者" (mid- to long-term resident); just check the expiry date matches your residence card.
- **電話番号 (phone number)**: a number where you can be reached during the day. The ward office calls if there is a problem with your application.
- **点字 (Braille)**: tick only if you want Braille added to the card; usually leave it blank.
- **顔写真貼付欄 (photo box)**: attach a **4.5cm tall × 3.5cm wide** photo. It must be taken within the last 6 months, facing forward, with no hat and a plain background, with your name and date of birth written on the back.
- **申請日・申請者氏名 (application date and applicant's name)**: write the application date and sign your name by hand.
- **The "□" for electronic certificates**: **do not black this out**. Only black out the "□" if you do not want electronic certificates issued. The form notes that electronic certificates are needed to use the card as a health insurance card, print residence certificates at convenience stores, file online with e-Tax and so on, and log in to マイナポータル (Mynaportal), so I recommend keeping them.
- **代理人記載欄 (proxy section)**: only for applicants aged 15 or under or adults under guardianship; usually leave blank.
- **事務処理記載欄 (office use)**: filled in by ward office staff; ignore it.

Once filled in, put the form in the enclosed return envelope and post it to the address on the envelope.

### Method 2: Online (recommended)

Applying online is simpler than by post, and you do not have to send the form back. Just scan the QR code on the notification or application form with your phone (it already contains your **application form ID**), register your email address, upload your photo, and enter your date of birth and other details as instructed to complete the application online.

After you submit it, the application is passed to your ward office, and the rest of the process is the same as applying by post.

## 4. Receiving the Collection Notice

About **a month** after applying, you receive a "**マイナンバーカード交付のお知らせ**" (notice of My Number Card issue) from the ward office.

![Envelope of the "マイナンバーカード交付のお知らせ" from the Shibuya ward residents' registry, marked "転送不要" and "親展"](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/IMG_6623.JPEG)
*The "マイナンバーカード交付のお知らせ" envelope: marked "転送不要" (do not forward) and "親展" (confidential), sent by the ward office directly to your registered address*

Two things on the envelope are worth noting:

- **転送不要 (do not forward)**: even if you have set up mail forwarding with the post office, this letter is not forwarded; it goes straight back to the ward office. So after moving, be sure to update your resident registration promptly.
- **親展 (confidential)**: to be opened by the addressee only.

The bottom of the envelope also lists an enquiry number; for Shibuya it is the "Shibuya My Number call centre, 0120-56-0523 (weekdays 8:30–17:15)".

Inside is a "**collection notice (交付通知書)**" postcard and documents explaining how to collect the card.

![My Number Card collection notice postcard showing the collection deadline and municipality, with a privacy seal at the bottom](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/IMG_6625.JPEG)
*The collection notice postcard: peel off the privacy seal (目隠しシール) to see where to collect the card; the back lists the documents to bring*

The postcard contains:

- **受取期限 (collection deadline)**: the deadline for collecting your card; collect it before then.
- **市区町村名・交付場所 (municipality and collection place)**: where to collect the card. The place is covered by a "**privacy seal (目隠しシール)**"; peel it off to see it.
- **Notes on the privacy seal**: if a proxy is collecting for you, you must **first** write your PIN on the back of the postcard, **then** peel off the seal and stick it over the PIN field to hide it. If you collect in person, just peel it off.
- **Back**: lists the documents to bring when collecting the card.
- **Small print at the bottom**: electronic certificates can only use characters that ordinary computers can display; if your name has characters that cannot be displayed, other characters are used instead, and you can ask for a change at the collection counter if needed.

After receiving the collection notice, you can book a date and time to collect the card. In Shibuya, collection **requires a booking**, made through the online booking system or by calling the number above.

---

## 5. Collecting the My Number Card at the Ward Office

The documents to bring on collection day are as listed on the back of the postcard; for Shibuya they include:

- The enclosed "マイナンバーカードの受け取りについて" (about collecting your My Number Card) document
- The **collection notice** (postcard)
- **ID** (such as your **residence card**)

### Collection steps

1. Bring the postcard to the ward office and tell the staff you want to collect your My Number Card.
2. The staff take you to the designated counter, where you sit and wait to be called.
3. When called, hand over the postcard and other documents. The staff give you a small slip to write down the **PINs (暗証番号)** you want to set.
4. The staff set up the card with the PINs you wrote.
5. When they hand you the card, **check the name, address, date of birth, period of stay and other details on the card on the spot**. If everything is correct, you are done.

### PINs: you must remember them

The PINs you set when collecting the card are very important. You need them later to sign electronically with the card, log in to online services, print residence certificates at convenience stores and so on. The My Number Card has four PINs in total:

| PIN type | Format | Main use |
|---------|------|---------|
| Signature electronic certificate | 6–16 letters and numbers (letters in upper case) | Submitting electronic documents online, e.g. e-Tax |
| User verification electronic certificate | 4 digits | Logging in to マイナポータル, printing residence certificates at convenience stores, etc. |
| Basic resident register | 4 digits | Procedures such as moving or changing your address or name |
| Card information input support | 4 digits | Online services reading the information on the card |

The three 4-digit PINs can be set to **the same number**, so in practice you usually only need to remember two: the "4 digits" and the "letters and numbers".

> <span data-icon="mingcute:warning-line"></span> Entering a 4-digit PIN wrong **3 times** in a row, or the signature PIN wrong **5 times** in a row, locks it, and you usually have to go to the ward office in person to reset it. When collecting the card, you can write the PINs on a piece of paper and keep it somewhere safe.

## 6. Reading the Front of the My Number Card

Once you have the card, take a moment to look at the details on it. Here is an explanation using the official sample card:

![Front of a sample My Number Card, printed with the name "番号 花子", address, date of birth, expiry date, electronic certificate expiry field, additional notes field and organ donation field](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-8-23490520.png)
*Front of a sample My Number Card: the personal number is not printed on the front; the 12-digit number is on the back*

- **氏名・住所・性別 (name, address, sex)**: your name, registered address and sex. Foreign residents' names are usually shown in Latin letters, matching the spelling on the residence card.
- **"平成元年 3月31日生"**: date of birth (the sample uses the Japanese calendar; Heisei 1 is 1989).
- **"2025年 3月31日まで有効"**: the expiry date of the card itself. For foreign residents it is usually the same as the expiry of the period of stay; after renewing your period of stay, remember to update it at the ward office.
- **"□□市長"**: the mayor of the issuing municipality.
- **"電子証明書の有効期限" field**: the expiry date of the electronic certificates on the card. This is calculated separately from the card's own expiry, so keep an eye on it too.
- **The dotted blank in the middle (追記欄, additional notes field)**: when your address or name changes, the ward office adds the new details here. For example, after moving and registering your move-in (see #6), the staff write your new address here, with no need to reissue the card.
- **Photo**: the photo you submitted when applying.
- **"臓器提供意思" field at the bottom**: for indicating your wishes on organ donation; whether to fill it in and sign is up to you.
- **16 letters and numbers at the bottom left**: the card's serial number, different for every card; it changes if the card is reissued.
- **The 4 digits next to it**: the security code, mainly used when reading the card's data with dedicated software.

> <span data-icon="mingcute:warning-line"></span> The 12-digit personal number is printed on the **back** of the card. Apart from legally required procedures such as tax and social security, do not let people copy or photograph the back of the card.

## Summary

Applying for a My Number Card is not complicated, but because the application form is posted to your home and you cannot pick it up at the ward office the same day, it takes about **six weeks** from filling in the form to actually getting the card. Key points:

- Tell the staff you want to apply when registering your move-in; you will then receive the **personal number notification** and **application form**
- The personal number notification is **never reissued**, so keep it safe
- You can apply **by post** or **online**; online is simpler, with no need to send the form back
- **Do not black out** the electronic certificate "□", or you will not be able to use services such as printing residence certificates at convenience stores
- About a month later you receive the **collection notice**; some ward offices require a **booking** to collect the card
- You must remember the **PINs** set when collecting the card; too many wrong attempts will lock them
- The personal number is printed on the **back** of the card; do not let people copy or photograph it

With this card, all sorts of procedures in Japan become much easier, so I recommend applying as soon as possible.

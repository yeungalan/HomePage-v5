# 01 | Studying in Japan #1: Arriving at Haneda Airport — The Full Entry Process

The first step on arriving in Tokyo is getting through entry procedures at Haneda Airport. For anyone entering Japan on a student visa for the first time, the process may be a little more involved than expected, but with proper preparation it goes smoothly overall. Here is my full experience entering at Haneda, which I hope helps anyone about to go to Japan.

## Before You Leave

Before departure, make sure you have the following documents with you:

- **Certificate of Eligibility** (在留資格認定証明書): applied for by your Japanese language school, and the core document for immigration.
- **Passport**: must be valid for your whole period of study.
- If you are carrying medication that has to be declared, you must also apply in advance to Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare for an **import permit for the medication**. The application must be done several days before departure, so do not put it off.

![Application for confirmation of pharmaceutical imports](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10879223.png)
*Bringing controlled medication into Japan requires getting the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's import confirmation in advance; enter Haneda Airport as the destination*

## Boarding

At check-in, because I had a one-way ticket, the ground staff specifically checked my visa documents and only issued my boarding pass once everything was confirmed. Keep this in mind: one-way passengers often get stricter checks.

I flew ANA. On the day, there was already a long queue at the counters, and most people seemed to be travellers like me dragging big suitcases.

![ANA check-in counters with a long queue of passengers](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10150065.png)
*The ANA check-in hall: one-way passengers should tell the ground staff up front that they hold a student visa, so the boarding pass is issued smoothly*

After checking in, I headed to the gate. ANA's gate area was spacious and tidy, and the wait felt fairly relaxed.

![ANA boarding area at gate S8](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10171835.png)

## Immigration

After landing, I followed most passengers to the immigration (入国審査) area. The process:

1. **Automated gates (fingerprints and face recognition)**: after this, go to a regular inspection counter.
2. **Staffed counter**: tell the officer you are entering on a student visa and hand over your Certificate of Eligibility. After checking your documents, the officer stamps your passport and issues your **residence card**.

Note that on a newly issued residence card, the "address" field usually says "not yet determined" for now. This is normal; it is updated with your actual address after you register your address (move-in registration).

After immigration, standing in the hall with my passport and brand-new residence card, I truly realised that this journey had officially begun.

![Holding a US passport and residence card in the Haneda arrivals hall](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10805599.png)
*Immigration done, passport and residence card in hand, with the bustling Haneda arrivals hall behind*

## Customs Declaration

Because I was carrying controlled medication, after immigration the customs officers checked my documents a second time, and I waited about **45 minutes**. They checked the medication import permit I had applied for in advance and let me through once it was confirmed.

On entry you must fill in Japan's customs "**Declaration of Accompanied and Unaccompanied Articles**" (携帯品・別送品申告書), and every item must be answered truthfully.

![Japanese customs declaration form](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10587225.png)
*The Japanese customs declaration form: if you have "unaccompanied articles" (luggage shipped separately), tick item 3 to declare them and keep a copy for later*

If you have nothing to declare, customs is usually quick for most travellers. But if you have anything to declare, be sure to declare it honestly and have the relevant documents ready to avoid unnecessary trouble.

## Postscript: The Unaccompanied Baggage Customs Saga

Besides my carry-on luggage, I had shipped a batch of personal belongings from Seattle to Tokyo by DHL. Soon after arriving, though, I found the DHL tracking page showing "**Shipment is on hold — Awaiting Import License**".

![DHL tracking page showing "Shipment is on hold"](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10223017.png)
*The DHL tracking page showing the shipment held because it was "Awaiting Import License to complete clearance"*

Then I received an email from DHL Japan asking for an "**import permit**" and explaining that if the shipment was declared as "Unaccompanied Baggage", about **5,000 yen** in duties and fees could be waived.

![Email from DHL Japan asking for an import permit](https://s3.alanyeung.co/blogphoto/ImagePaste%202026-4-10321204.png)
*DHL Japan's email: if you declared "unaccompanied articles" on the customs form at entry, in theory these fees can be waived*

I only found out afterwards that if you correctly declare "unaccompanied articles" (item 3) on the customs form at entry and keep a copy of the declaration, you can submit it to DHL as proof and have the fees waived. If you plan to ship luggage to Japan, **I strongly recommend learning the unaccompanied baggage declaration process in advance** and handling it properly at customs on entry to save yourself the hassle later.

## Summary

The whole entry process is: boarding check → immigration (fingerprints, photo, document check) → receiving the residence card → customs declaration. The process itself is not complicated, but pay attention to the documents needed at each step. Travellers carrying special medication in particular should apply for permission in advance and allow extra waiting time.

Arriving at Haneda and setting foot in Japan, student life officially begins.
